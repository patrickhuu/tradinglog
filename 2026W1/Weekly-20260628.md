The rules that govern early assignment for US single stock options are partially random, but the entire process actually happens in a two-tiered system. You aren't assigned directly by the market; instead, the assignment flows down from a clearinghouse to your specific broker. Here is exactly how the rule works and how you get selected:

Tier 1: The OCC to the Broker (Random)

When an options holder decides to exercise early, their broker submits an exercise notice to the Options Clearing Corporation (OCC), which clears all US equity options. The OCC collects all the exercise notices for a specific option series at the end of the day. To decide which brokerage firms get hit with those assignments, the OCC uses a randomized allocation procedure. They place all open short positions onto an automated "wheel" in sequential order. The system calculates a random starting point and a mathematical skip interval to select positions. This ensures that no single broker is unfairly targeted, and the distribution across clearing firms is completely randomized.

Tier 2: The Broker to You (Random or Sequential)

Once your broker is assigned a batch of contracts by the OCC, it is up to them to decide which of their short-selling clients gets assigned. FINRA and the options exchanges require brokers to use a "fair and impartial" method, but brokers are allowed to choose between two main systems: Random Selection: The broker runs an internal lottery where every short contract held by their clients has an equal mathematical chance of being chosen. (e.g., Robinhood, Webull, and Tradier primarily use random allocation).First-In, First-Out (FIFO): This is sequential. The broker assigns the contracts to the clients who have held their short positions the longest. If you sold your call or put weeks ago, you will be assigned before someone who sold theirs yesterday. (e.g., some institutional or traditional brokers offer FIFO).

Note: Some brokers even use a pro-rata allocation (allocating a proportional percentage of the assignment to all short holders), though Random and FIFO are the most common for retail traders.

4. 你在 SPX 支撐位判斷上有優勢，應集中火力在可重複的 edge。

**Key Lessons**

1. 不要為了多賺一點 premium 把可控風險變成大虧。
2. Roll 只能在風險/報酬更好時做，不是拖延。
3. 個股交易要降倉或停做，避免 margin/assignment 逼迫變形操作。

**Key Principles**

1. 先活下來：寧可少賺，不可全虧。
2. 只做有明確 edge 的場景。
3. 先設規則再交易：何時減倉、何時roll、何時直接退出。
